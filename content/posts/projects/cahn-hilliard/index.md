After gaining interest in dynamical systems through my [work on the Navier-Stokes equations]({{< ref "content/posts/projects/cahn-hilliard.md" >}}), I pursued additional experience researching dynamical systems and theoretical PDEs, leading to my participation in [Prof. Ryan Goh's](https://math.bu.edu/people/rgoh/) REU at Boston University (summer 2026). There, I studied the 1D Cahn-Hilliard equation (CHE) – as derived from the 2D Swift-Hohenberg equation – under a slowly varying parameter. I examined the non-autonomous dynamics according to a phenomenological result that describes a “freezing-out” behavior of kinks in the system, working with a teammate to determine the selected pattern of defects at freeze-out time. I proved novel theoretical results, including the existence of a “pullback” attractor to the non-autonomous CHE. We plan to publish our completed results in a peer-reviewed, mathematical physics journal. Currently, we have a 50-page manuscript describing the non-autonomous evolution for small domains, with concrete steps advancing the same classifications to large domains.
