With increased confidence in mathematically modeling natural systems, I pursued a project with direct applications to a field outside mathematics. From taking graduate coursework in dynamical systems and chaos theory, I built rapport with [Prof. Dora Velcsov at UNO](https://www.unomaha.edu/college-of-arts-and-sciences/mathematics/about-us/directory/dora-velcsov.php). The following semester, I took her class in Boolean networks, coincidentally alongside a biology course taught by a local bat ecologist, Prof. Jeremy White. 

I learned more about the catastrophic fungus causing white-nose syndrome (WNS) in bats, which provide enormously beneficial pest control for farmland. Driven to determine what factors most exacerbate the spread of WNS, I spearheaded a project connecting the disparate fields of Boolean networks and ecology. I built a probabilistic Boolean network from scratch, with over a dozen parameters derived from a thorough literature search across biology, epidemiology, and bat ecology. 

My model weighs the effect of these parameters on population susceptibility and compares theorized immune responses. At the conclusion of the graduate course, Prof. Velcsov secured funding for a classmate and I to make this ecosystem analysis publication quality.By Dec. 2026, we will submit our findings to Ecological Modelling, a high-quality, peer-reviewed journal in its subject. In 2027, we will present our findings at local math conferences and the North American Society for Bat Research. I will also make the model free, open-source, and publically available.
